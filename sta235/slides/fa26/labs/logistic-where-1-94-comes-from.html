Where 1.94 comes from

We ran a logistic regression of sex on stated height and got a slope of 0.66. The usual interpretation is that each extra inch multiplies the odds of being male by e0.66 = 1.94. This page shows why the exponential appears, and why the answer is the same at every height.

Step 2

The derivation, one line at a time

Call the log odds at your starting height A. It holds everything about where you start: the intercept and your height. Watch what happens to it.

All five steps shown

  1. Write the odds at both heights

    odds(h) = e−44.45 + 0.66·h   odds(h + 1) = e−44.45 + 0.66·(h + 1)
    Odds are e raised to the log odds. That's just undoing the log on the left side of the model.

  2. Split the new exponent into "old" plus "extra"

    −44.45 + 0.66·(h + 1) = (−44.45 + 0.66·h) + 0.66 = A + 0.66
    Distribute the 0.66 across (h + 1). The part in purple is exactly the old log odds, A. One more inch adds 0.66 on the log-odds scale, and nothing else.

  3. Adding in an exponent means multiplying outside it

    odds(h + 1) = eA + 0.66 = eA × e0.66
    This is the exponent rule ea + b = ea × eb. It's the same rule as 23 × 22 = 25, which is 8 × 4 = 32. And eA is just the old odds.

  4. Divide new odds by old odds

    odds(h + 1)odds(h) = eA × e0.66eA
    A ratio asks "how many times bigger?", which is the natural question once the change is a multiplication.

  5. The starting point cancels

    eA × e0.66eA = e0.66 = 1.94
    eA is on the top and the bottom, so it divides out. Everything about where you started is gone: the intercept, your height, your starting odds. Only the coefficient is left, which is why the multiplier is the same at every height.

Step 3

See it on a slide rule

Here are three scales lined up. Height is evenly spaced and so is log odds, because log odds is a straight line in height. The odds scale is logarithmic: 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10 and 100 sit at equal distances, so equal distances mean equal ratios.

Drag the orange band. Its width is a fixed number of inches, so on the middle scale it always spans the same amount of log odds. On the bottom scale, the same width is always the same multiplication.

Taking the log turns multiplication into addition, so a fixed step on the log scale is a fixed ratio on the original scale. Logistic regression is linear on the log-odds scale, which means it's multiplicative on the odds scale.

Step 4

More than one inch: the multipliers stack

Two inches add 0.66 twice in the log odds, so the odds get multiplied by 1.94 twice. In general, k inches give e0.66·k = 1.94k. It compounds like interest.
