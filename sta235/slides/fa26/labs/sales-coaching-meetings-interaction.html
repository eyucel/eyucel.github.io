Does coaching change the value of a client meeting?

A sales model with an interaction lets the meeting-to-sales relationship be different for coached and uncoached salespeople.

An interaction is a “depends on” effect. Here, the sales impact of one more meeting depends on whether the salesperson received coaching. On a graph, an interaction appears as non-parallel lines.

The model

Let Coaching equal 1 for a coached salesperson and 0 for a salesperson with no coaching. Sales are measured in thousands of dollars per month.

Sales = 35 + 2.5(Meetings) + 8(Coaching) + 1.0(Coaching × Meetings)
No coaching (Coaching = 0)Sales = 35 + 2.5(Meetings)
The meeting slope is 2.5.
Coaching (Coaching = 1)Sales = 43 + 3.5(Meetings)
The meeting slope is 2.5 + 1.0 = 3.5.

How to interpret the interaction coefficient

It is the difference in the two meeting slopes. If the interaction coefficient is +1, then each extra meeting adds $1k more for coached salespeople than for uncoached salespeople. It is not the total coaching effect: the coaching difference at m meetings is Coaching coefficient + Interaction coefficient × m.
