When one slope changes continuously with another variable

Suppose a retailer models weekly revenue using digital ad spending and the discount rate. An interaction allows the payoff from another $1,000 of ads to change as the discount changes.

A quantitative-by-quantitative interaction is a changing-slope model. There is not one ad-spending slope. There is a different slope for every discount rate.

Start with the model, then collect the slope

Revenue and ad spending are measured in thousands of dollars; discount is measured in percentage points.

Revenue = 40 + 3(Ads) + 1.2(Discount) + 0.18(Ads x Discount)
Ad slope

At discount d, the ad slope is b1 + b3d. This is the main quantity to watch.

Discount slope

At ad spending a, the discount slope is b2 + b3a. The relationship works in either direction.

Interaction

b3 is the change in the ad slope for each one-point increase in discount.

How to read a quantitative interaction

The product term changes a slope; it does not replace either main effect.

1 · Choose a moderator value

To interpret the ad effect, first choose a discount rate such as 10%.

2 · Compute the conditional slope

Insert that discount into b1 + b3d. With the defaults: 3 + .18(10) = 4.8.

3 · Compare slopes

Two discount rates that differ by 5 points have ad slopes that differ by 5b3. When b3 = 0, all slices are parallel.

The values are simulated for teaching. Association from this fitted model is not automatically a causal effect.
