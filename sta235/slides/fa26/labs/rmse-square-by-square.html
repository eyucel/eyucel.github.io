Building models for prediction · Apple quarterly revenue

RMSE, square by square

We fit a revenue model to Apple's first 54 quarters and held back the 4 most recent. Then we asked the model to forecast those 4 quarters it had never seen. RMSPE (root mean squared prediction error) boils the four misses down to one number in dollars.

RMSPE = $4.95B A typical forecast is off by about $4.95 billion.

Drag the blue forecasts, or type new ones in the table, and every step below updates.

Step 1

Four forecasts, four misses

Each error is the actual revenue minus the forecast, Y − Ŷ. A positive error means the model forecast too low. A negative one means it forecast too high.

Step 2

Square each miss

Squaring does two jobs. It makes every error positive, so misses in opposite directions can't cancel out. And it makes big misses count for a lot more than small ones. Below, each error is drawn as a real square with that side length, so its area is the squared error.

Percentages are each square's share of the total squared error.

Step 3

Average the squares, then take the side

Add the four areas and divide by 4. That's the mean squared prediction error (MSPE), the area of the "average square" drawn in green. Its units are billions of dollars squared, which nobody can picture. Taking the square root gives the side length of that average square, back in plain billions of dollars. That side is the RMSPE.

Read RMSPE like the residual standard error: smaller is better. The difference is that RSE measures how well the model fits data it was trained on, while RMSPE measures how well it predicts data it never saw.

Step 4

Same RMSPE, different stories

Squaring means RMSPE cares a lot about the single worst miss. Load each scenario and compare RMSPE with the mean absolute error (MAE), which averages the misses without squaring them.

ScenarioErrors ($B)RMSPEMAE

"Off by $5B every quarter" and "perfect except one $10B miss" have the same RMSPE, $5B. One $10B miss is a square of area 100, the same total area as four $5B squares (4 × 25). MAE rates the single miss as half as bad. RMSPE says a forecast that's occasionally way off is as costly as one that's always a little off.

In R

The one-line version

predictions <- exp(predict(apple_model, test)) # exp because the model predicts log(Revenue) actuals <- test$Revenue sqrt(mean((predictions - actuals)^2)) [1] 4.947

Read it from the inside out: errors, then ^2 to square them, then mean, then sqrt. Those are the four steps above.

Forecasts and actuals are the test-set values from the model log(Revenue) ~ Period + Quarter + COVID + log(lag1), fit to periods 1–54 and tested on periods 55–58. Revenue is in billions of dollars. Companion lab: Train, Test, Repeat.
